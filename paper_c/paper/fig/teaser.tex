\captionof{figure}{\textbf{Overexposed facts surface when the rule changes.} (a) Under the official rule, smoking alone makes this
scene level one, so every other fact---including \emph{open flame}---has zero exposure: the verdict carries no information about
it. (b) After smoking is downgraded to level two, a model trained on verdicts reads the cigarette as an open flame (0.95) and keeps
the scene at level one; a direct model told the new rule does the same. The untrained model is correct. (c) \lux reads calibrated
facts (open flame 0.00) and, with the rule table edited and no retraining, outputs level two, matching the blind human label.
Over all facts, verdict training raises the false-positive rate on clipped cells from 0.14 to 0.23--0.30; \lux: 0.05.}
